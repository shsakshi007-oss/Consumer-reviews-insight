    26.5% of low-rated reviews are about shade fit, rising to 36.6% among 1–2 star reviews

    Step 3 · How the numbers were produced

    Built with Python and NLP, tested for accuracy

    1. Collected the 1–3 star reviews from Nykaa’s public review pages with Python, keeping rating, shade, title, text and date, and removed duplicates.
    2. Counted repeated two- and three-word phrases to see what buyers actually write, then grouped them into 13 complaint themes.
    3. Tagged each review with keyword rules that handle negation, so “not good coverage” is a complaint and “good coverage” is praise.
    4. Clustered the reviews (TF-IDF and K-means) to find wording the rules missed. Untagged reviews fell from 336 to 252, most of them praise-only 3-star reviews.
    5. Compared the tags with 100 reviews labelled by hand.

    How accurate is the model?

    Python tags checked against 100 reviews labelled by hand. Bar = F1 score (balance of precision and recall), out of 100%.

    Shade fit18 reviews in sample68%P 65% · R 72%
    Coverage15 reviews in sample73%P 73% · R 73%
    How it wears18 reviews in sample78%P 92% · R 67%

    Precision (P): of the reviews tagged with a problem, the share that really mention it. Recall (R): of the reviews that mention it, the share tagged. F1 = 2·P·R ÷ (P + R). Small samples, so treat these as ±10 points. Shares are of 1–3 star reviews, not all buyers.

    Step 6 · What the brand can do

    Three fixes, cheapest first

    Each targets one problem family. The pink line on each card is the goal: the share of 1–3 star reviews that mention that problem today, and where it should fall in new reviews within two quarters. Effort levels and targets are my estimates.

    Fix 1

    Fix the Nykaa listing

    Owner: e-commerce · Effort: low

    Shades in light-to-dark order with real-skin swatches, a three-question shade finder, and how-to-apply and skin-type tips on the product page.

    Evidence: shade fit in 190 of 716 reviews; 53 reviewers give each other shade advice.

    Goal: reviews citing shade fit, 26.5% today → under 15%

    Fix 2

    An education campaign: “You might be using it wrong”

    Owner: brand, social and partnerships · Effort: medium

    Coverage is praised in 90 reviews and criticised in 116, so the same formula works for some buyers and not others. Part of that gap is likely technique. The campaign teaches it in the buyers’ own words, through people they already trust to teach makeup. Shade and oxidation problems need Fix 1 and Fix 3 instead.

    1. Problem-first reels. Each reel opens with a complaint buyers actually write (cakey, creasing under the eyes, not enough coverage), then a creator fixes it on camera with this concealer. The brand page reposts them as one series.
    2. Makeup academies. Partner with makeup academies on a masterclass and put the concealer in student kits. Students learn to use it properly, then use it on clients, including brides and their families.
    3. Academy reels. Academies post class and bridal reels all the time. A tagged product in those reels is a professional endorsement, not an ad.

    Evidence: coverage short of the claim in 116 reviews, yet good coverage praised in 90. Creasing, caking and blending come up in 91 reviews.

    Goal: reviews citing low coverage, 16.2% today → under 10%

    Fix 3

    Test the formula

    Owner: R&D · Effort: high

    Lab-test the oxidation and cakiness reports. Reformulate only if tests confirm them, since some may come from how it’s applied.

    Evidence: how it wears (cakey, creasing, blending, oxidation, texture, lasting) in 119 reviews.

    Goal: reviews citing wear issues, 16.6% today → under 12%
